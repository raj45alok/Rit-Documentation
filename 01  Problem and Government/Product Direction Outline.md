## 1. Project Intent

Rit is an ambitious project with the broad objective of **promoting sports participation, physical fitness, healthy lifestyles and better nutrition among the youth of India**.

However, the initial idea is intentionally broad.

The objective at this stage is **not to immediately build an application**.

Instead, the first objective is to understand the problem deeply and determine:

- What are the most important problems faced by Indian youth?
- Which problems are significant enough to solve?
- Which problems are poorly addressed by existing solutions?
- Which users are most affected?
- What solutions already exist?
- Where are the gaps in the existing ecosystem?
- Where can technology create meaningful value?
- Where can AI genuinely improve the solution?
- What is technically and financially feasible?
- What is the smallest useful product we can build to validate the idea?

The final product should emerge from this research.

> **The research decides the product — not the other way around.**

Rit will follow a **problem-first and evidence-driven product-development philosophy**.

       ↓

The goal is to build something that **solves a real problem**, not simply something that demonstrates a collection of technologies.

## 6.1 Age / Developmental-Stage Segmentation

Rit's primary target (ages 6–16) additionally requires segmentation by **developmental stage**, not just demographic category, because trainability, motivation, and appropriate content differ sharply by age even within "youth." This should be researched against established frameworks such as Long-Term Athlete Development (LTAD):

- **Ages 6–9** — fundamental movement skills, agility/balance/coordination, multi-sport exposure, fun over structure or analytics.
- **Ages 9–12** — the widest window for learning sport-specific skills across multiple sports; still avoid premature specialization.
- **Ages 12–16** — major fitness development, tactical understanding, gradual and deliberate specialization.

Any product feature set, pricing tier, or content plan for a given age band should be validated against what is developmentally appropriate for that band, not assumed from adult fitness-app conventions.

For each segment we will investigate:

- Goals
- Problems
- Current behavior
- Existing solutions
- Constraints
- Motivation
- Technology usage
- Willingness to adopt a new solution

The eventual MVP should have a **specific primary user**, rather than attempting to serve everyone.

# 15. Problem Hypotheses

Research should produce several possible problem hypotheses.

For example:

### Hypothesis A

> Young people want to participate in sports but have difficulty finding suitable people to play with.

### Hypothesis B

> Young people start fitness programs but struggle to remain consistent.

### Hypothesis C

> College students lack affordable and practical nutrition guidance.

### Hypothesis D

> Youth have difficulty discovering accessible sports facilities and activities.

### Hypothesis E

> Existing fitness platforms focus heavily on individual tracking while social/community participation remains underserved.

### Hypothesis F

> India's grassroots-to-elite sports pipeline is not primarily a talent-scarcity or infrastructure-only problem. It is a **leaky pipeline**: talented children are identified but fail to progress (government data shows only ~12–15% of sub-junior identified athletes reach state-level/advanced training), and a major contributing cause is an undertrained, uncertified grassroots coaching / PE-teacher base that cannot reliably identify or nurture talent early. A product that strengthens early-stage identification, structured age-appropriate development, and continuity of progression could address a gap that is currently underserved by both government programs (implementation-limited) and existing fitness apps (not sport-progression-focused).

These are examples only.

They must be validated.

## 15.1 Evidence-Backed Problem Statements (Research Pass 1 — Aug 2026)

Following the first full research pass (Perplexity discovery across six threads: youth physical activity, nutrition, coaching quality, state-wise variation, competitor landscape, parental drivers), the hypotheses above were synthesized into problem statements with explicit evidence-strength ratings. Two independent synthesis passes (Claude in-conversation, and a separate Master Agent research analysis) were cross-checked and merged below. **Evidence strength is rated separately from product priority** — a problem being well-evidenced does not automatically mean it is the right one to build first, and a problem being unmeasured does not mean it is a strong opportunity by default.

| Problem Statement | Evidence strength | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Grassroots coaching / talent-ID quality gap** — inconsistent PE-teacher and coach qualifications, no national licensing or standardized talent-ID training | **Strong** | Confirmed directly by India's Dec 2025 government coaching task-force report; consistent with leaky-pipeline data (~12–15% sub-junior→state progression) |
| **2. Girls face a structural, not motivational, participation gap** | **Strong** | Multiple independent national/regional datasets (CNNS, Pune, multi-site 2024) converge; mechanism (safety/mobility restriction) is specifically evidenced, not inferred |
| **3. Rising sedentary behavior alongside insufficient activity** | **Moderate–strong** | National *direction* (India Report Card grade fell C→C-→D- 2016–2022) is clear; exact prevalence numbers vary widely by study/method |
| **4. Nutrition guidance mismatched to India's actual (biomarker-confirmed) deficiency profile** | **Strong** (for the deficiency data) / **Moderate** (for what intervention fixes it) | CNNS biomarker data is high-quality national evidence (anaemia 24–28%, zinc up to 32%, B12 up to 31%, folate up to 37%); protein-centric messaging is not evidence-supported |
| **5. Absence of a continuity mechanism from initial interest to sustained progression** | **Moderate** | Strong on describing what correlates with state-level outcomes (Haryana/Maharashtra's structural features); weaker on proving causality — no controlled comparison exists |
| **6. Cost/logistics friction suppresses consistency more than initial enrollment** | **Moderate** | Directionally consistent across multiple local/regional sources; no single national Indian study quantifies this cleanly |
| **7. Parents have no credible way to verify program/coach quality before paying** | **Strong** (as a market-structure fact) | Directly observed across a 14-platform competitor scan — no platform has independently verified outcomes |
| **8. "Sports retention" / dropout as a quantified national problem** | **Unvalidated — data gap, not a strong signal** | India has no reliable national dropout/retention dataset for ages 6–16. This is an *absence of evidence*, not evidence of a large problem. Should not be treated as "Very High priority" on the strength of being unmeasured — needs primary research (interviews) before being weighted heavily. |
| **9. Peer/community participation as a retention lever** | **Moderate** | Real association evidence (active friends correlate with +32 daily MVPA minutes, larger for girls) but observational/cross-sectional — supports design choices (buddy enrollment, team formats), not yet a standalone product thesis |
| **10. School–parent–coach coordination/fragmentation** | **Plausible, not yet evidenced** | Structurally logical given the multi-stakeholder ecosystem, but not directly measured in any research pass so far — needs primary validation before being treated as a core problem |

### Strategic direction — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Team Rit has explicitly decided:

1. **Rit will stay gender-neutral.** The girls' structural-participation-gap hypothesis (Problem Statement 2) — despite being the most robustly evidenced single finding across the research base (national CNNS data + independent regional studies) — is a deliberate, acknowledged trade-off, not the founding wedge. Universal safety/inclusion should remain a baseline design principle for all users regardless.
2. **The core thesis is fixing systemic fragmentation through technology** — i.e., the participation/coordination ecosystem direction (Problem Statements 1, 5, and 10: coaching-quality/talent-ID gap, absent continuity mechanism between interest and progression, and school–parent–coach coordination), rather than a pure training-content subscription library.

This supersedes the "not yet decided" framing previously in this section. Next steps: translate this into concrete user segments and a specific technology-bridging mechanism (Sections 16–18).

### MVP wedge — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Of the three technology-bridging mechanisms considered (coaching-quality/talent-ID layer; continuity/progression tracker; parent-coach-school coordination layer), Team Rit has chosen to sequence rather than build all three at once, consistent with the MVP philosophy in Section 22:

1. **Phase 1 (MVP wedge): Coaching-quality / talent-ID layer.** Directly targets Problem Statement 1 — the most strongly evidenced problem in the research base (confirmed by India's Dec 2025 government coaching task-force report). Rit becomes infrastructure that standardizes how coaches assess, track, and develop a child's ability, addressing the "no standardized talent-ID training, no licensing/quality assurance" gap.
2. **Phase 2: Continuity/progression tracker** (Problem Statement 5) — layered on top of Phase 1's assessment data, once a base of coaches/children exists to track over time.
3. **Phase 3: Parent-coach-school coordination layer** (Problem Statement 10) — the most crowded/least differentiated of the three per the competitor scan, deferred until Rit has established a data/trust foundation via Phases 1–2.

Rationale for starting with A rather than B or C: it has the strongest independent evidence, is least crowded competitively (no platform in the competitor scan offers validated, standardized talent-ID/coach-quality tooling), and produces the underlying data (assessment history, progression records) that Phases 2 and 3 would depend on anyway.

### MVP build sequence — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Within the Phase 1 wedge (coaching-quality/talent-ID layer), Team Rit has defined a more granular build order:

1. **Coach module** — training and credentialing pathway for coaches, structured around the government's proposed tiered accreditation framework (Community/PE-teacher → Grassroots → Intermediate → Elite → National, per the Dec 2025 coaching task-force report).
2. **Talent-identification module** — standardized SOPs for assessing and tracking a child's development, filling the gap that no current Indian platform (government or private) offers validated, standardized talent-ID methodology.
3. **Student onboarding / pre-initial training guide** — this is where AI-assisted video/biomechanics analysis enters, deliberately sequenced *after* the coach and talent-ID modules are built and credible, not as a launch-day headline feature. Rationale: several existing competitors (Peak Athlete, Durosport, KidPRO, Striide) already make unverified "AI-powered" claims; leading with the same claim before the underlying methodology is validated would undermine Rit's core differentiator (credible, standardized quality — Problem Statement 7). Once live, this phase also aims to make training sessions interactive/engaging for the child, not just diagnostic for the coach.

**Cross-cutting architecture decision:** three role-based access tiers — **Athlete, Coach, Parent/Administrator** — span all phases above, each with different permissions and views into the same underlying assessment/progression data.

### Talent-Identification Framework — decided (30 Aug 2026)

**Model: three-tier generic→specific, built on top of (not duplicating) Khelo India's existing free fitness battery.**

Khelo India already provides a generic *fitness* battery (BMI, Flamingo balance, plate tapping for ages 5–8; BMI, 50m sprint, 600m run, sit-and-reach, curl-up, push-ups for ages 9–18). This measures fitness, not sport-orientation — Rit's talent-ID layer sits on top of this gap, not in competition with it.

| Tier | Age band (LTAD stage) | Purpose | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 6–9 (FUNdamentals) | Orientation, not selection | Generic motor-skill battery (coordination, agility, balance, basic strength/power); no child excluded from anything at this stage — data just begins building a longitudinal profile |
| **2** | 9–12 (Learn-to-Train) | Sport-orientation | Repeat generic battery over time + light taster sessions across candidate sports; this is internationally the widest window for multi-sport skill acquisition |
| **3** | 12–16 (Train-to-Train) | Specialization | Sport-specific technical/tactical batteries once a sport is chosen |

Evidence basis: a Chinese study on elite youth athletes across six sports found a generic 25-item anthropometric/motor/physiological battery could correctly sort athletes into their actual sport with up to 98.9% accuracy — validating that meaningful sport-orientation is possible without sport-specific equipment at the early stage. The SOP must mandate **repeated observation over time** (e.g., quarterly re-testing), not a single-day snapshot, and must explicitly account for late developers — both points were repeatedly flagged as critical in the youth talent-ID literature and in India-specific pediatric research.

**Initial sport shortlist: Athletics, Badminton, Boxing, Swimming.** (Wrestling considered and deprioritized for this stage — 30 Aug 2026.)

Rationale:
- **Athletics, Badminton, Boxing** — align with India's demonstrated Olympic/Commonwealth medal strengths and skill profile (higher endurance, strength, durability; agility as a proven strength in badminton), and with the sports where SAI/Khelo India coaching and infrastructure density is already strongest — meaning a talent-ID pipeline has somewhere real to route a child (directly supports Problem Statement 5, continuity).
- **Swimming** — India's largest medal white-space among major Olympic categories (no Olympic swimming medal to date); fits FUNdamentals-stage science well (low-impact, builds coordination/cardiovascular base, avoids early specialization). **Caveat:** pool access is far scarcer and more unevenly distributed than grounds/courts, so swimming rollout will likely need to be sequenced city-by-city around partner-pool availability rather than launched uniformly alongside the other three sports.

**Swimming-specific SOP refinement (decided 30 Aug 2026):** pond/lake access is an acceptable substitute for pool access at the grassroots level — this is consistent with how much of India's existing swimming talent has historically developed, and removes facility availability as a hard blocker. Two precision requirements follow from this:

1. **Tiered measurement standard.** Tier 1–2 (FUNdamentals/Learn-to-Train, ages 6–12) assessment in ponds/lakes should be qualitative — water comfort, stroke technique, coordination — since open water lacks fixed markers and consistent conditions (current, depth) for reliable timed comparison across children or locations. Tier 3 (Train-to-Train, 12–16), where cross-child comparison and real specialization begin to matter, should move to standardized pool access (25m/50m lane) wherever feasible, since precise, comparable timing data becomes necessary at that stage.
2. **Open-water safety module.** The coach-credentialing module (Phase 1) must include an open-water-specific safety/safeguarding track — distinct from pool-based coaching credentials — covering supervision ratios, rescue certification, and site-specific risk assessment. This follows directly from the coaching research, which repeatedly flagged safeguarding as a top due-diligence area parents should be able to verify, and open water carries materially higher risk (no lifeguard infrastructure, unpredictable conditions) than a supervised pool.

### Tier 1/2 Test Battery Methodology — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Software-only, no hardware/wearables at this stage. Two validated, low-resource-appropriate batteries identified:

- **PERF-FIT** — purpose-built and validated for low-resource settings (the closest match to Rit's actual context); measures agility, power, balance, coordination, and reaction time using minimal equipment (cones, foam blocks, a sandbag, a stopwatch). Directly addresses the gap that most validated child motor-skill batteries were built for and normed on high-income-country populations.
- **KTK3+** — a leaner 4-item battery (jumping sideways, moving sideways, balancing backwards, plus a throw-catch coordination task) validated across the full 6–19 age range with normative data for both sexes. Its appeal: the same 4 items apply across the whole 6–16 target range, simplifying the longitudinal data model to one consistent metric per child rather than different tests at different ages.

The technology layer is a coach-facing app for manual score/time entry against a child's profile, auto-generating longitudinal trend and peer-percentile views — digitizing an currently-paper (or non-existent) process, with no wearables or computer vision required at this stage.

### Tier 3 Sport-Specific Test Content — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Final sport shortlist confirmed: **Athletics, Badminton, Boxing, Swimming.** Hardware constraint (no hardware/wearables at this stage) applies here too — two of the four sports have a hardware tension in the pure sport-science literature, resolved below with hardware-free proxies.

- **Athletics** — the **OSF test**: 3×10m shuttle run (speed/agility), standing broad jump (power), 1kg medicine ball overhead throw (strength), 4-min run (endurance). Validated, equipment-light (tape measure, stopwatch, medicine ball), with published age/sex normative percentile charts, and specifically designed to orient children toward event groups (sprints/jumps/throws/distance).
- **Badminton** — footwork/agility drills (four- or six-point footwork patterns using floor markers/cones), a badminton-specific agility T-test, and basic reaction-time drills. Validated and equipment-light; research specifically supports identifying tall, agile, high-endurance children via generic physical tests before sport-specific skill exposure.
- **Boxing** — **hardware tension.** The sport-specific literature (punch force, velocity, reaction time) is built almost entirely on instrumented gloves/bags, force plates, or IMU suits, which conflicts with the no-hardware constraint. **Resolution:** substitute the same generic power/agility/reaction items used elsewhere — medicine ball throw (upper-body power), standing broad jump (lower-body power), shuttle run (agility), simple reaction-ball catch drill (hand-eye reaction) — deferring true instrumented punch telemetry to a later, hardware-enabled phase.
- **Swimming** — **partial hardware tension.** Full swim-specific TID (in-water force sensors, camera-based stroke biomechanics) needs equipment Rit doesn't have yet. **Resolution:** two hardware-free pieces carry real evidence weight — anthropometry (height, arm span, sitting height via tape measure; arm-span-to-height ratio is a documented predictor) and dryland power tests (vertical jump, standing broad jump). Stroke technique is assessed qualitatively by the coach (manual stroke count over a marked distance, technique observation) — consistent with the pond/lake qualitative approach already defined for Tiers 1–2.

### FUNdamentals Stage Philosophy (Ages 6–9) — decided (30 Aug 2026)

The 6–9 age band (Tier 1) is explicitly about instilling **fitness and healthy-life habits**, not sport-specific selection — consistent with the LTAD FUNdamentals principle already established (Section 6.1: fun over structure, multi-sport exposure, no child excluded from anything at this stage). **Athletics is designated the first building-block sport** — its generic movement vocabulary (running, jumping, throwing) underpins the physical foundation the other three sports build on, making it the natural entry point before broader sport exposure begins at Tier 2. Beyond physical development, sport participation is valued as a vehicle for building resilience — teaching children to compete, lose, recover, and persist — a developmental outcome alongside physical fitness and talent orientation.

### Coach Credential Model — decided (30 Aug 2026)

**Do not build Rit as a competing accreditation authority.** As of Feb–June 2026, the Government of India is actively building a **National Coach Accreditation Board (NCAB)** (Task Force led by Pullela Gopichand), including a National Coach Registry, a dedicated Accreditation Portal, and standards explicitly described as **LTAD-based** — currently in pilot phase with 3–4 National Sports Federations, not yet nationally live. This is philosophically aligned with Rit's own LTAD-based framework.

**Decision:** Rit's coach module will be built as **NCAB-compatible infrastructure**, not a rival credentialing body — mapping directly onto NCAB's proposed tiers (Grassroots → Intermediate → Elite → National) and building a coach's assessment/training portfolio in a form that is ready to plug into NCAB once it rolls out nationally. This positions Rit as complementary to the incoming government system rather than exposed to future displacement by it, while still allowing Rit to build and operate ahead of NCAB's full national launch.

### Grassroots Coach Curriculum Structure — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Adopted from a cross-check of international entry-level coach-education frameworks (ISCF/ICCE-ASOIF, UK Coaching, England Athletics, England Squash, World Rugby, BWF), converging on a consistent stacked model. **Full drill-level curriculum content is a separate training-content deliverable, not reproduced in this strategic document — this section records the adopted structure only.**

- **Safeguarding is a mandatory prerequisite**, not optional add-on content — no coach is certified without it, consistent with every mature system reviewed.
- **Format:** 20–40 blended learning hours — self-paced online modules (safeguarding, child development, sport-specific rules) + tutor-led workshop (session planning, communication, ethics, scenarios) + supervised field practice (2–4 observed live sessions) + portfolio (session plans, risk assessments, reflections).
- **Assessment: competency-based, not attendance-based** — a "competent / not-yet-competent" decision via practical observation, with a defined remediation/reassessment path, not automatic certification for showing up.
- **Registry:** a publicly/organizationally verifiable coach ID recording qualification level, sport, expiry date, and safeguarding status — this is a direct, buildable software feature for Rit's coach module.
- **Progression pathway:** Assistant → Grassroots coach → Development coach → Advanced/Performance coach, mapping onto NCAB's proposed tiers.
- **Renewable license** (2–3 year validity), tied to safeguarding recertification and CPD credits — one-time certification is treated as inadequate in every mature system reviewed.

**India-specific curriculum additions (beyond generic imported frameworks):** heat/humidity/air-pollution/monsoon risk management; low-equipment and small-space session design; gender-sensitive coaching and girls' safe participation; child-safety and digital-communication (WhatsApp-era) boundaries; inclusion across disability, language, caste/community, income, and rural/urban access; parent communication around academic-pressure trade-offs, fees, and realistic sport-career expectations; prevention of early specialization, overtraining, and unsafe weight-control/supplement practices; referral procedures for suspected injury, anaemia/fatigue, undernutrition, and mental-health or safeguarding concerns; and — critically — **explicit, trained separation between fitness screening and talent selection, with safeguards against premature exclusion.** This last point directly reinforces the Tier 1 "orientation, not selection; no child excluded" principle already established in the Talent-Identification Framework above.

### Age-Band Delivery Methodology — decided (30 Aug 2026)

Sourced from World Athletics (Kids' Athletics), BWF Level 1, grassroots boxing federations (e.g. Boxing Scotland), and Swim England's Learn to Swim Framework, cross-checked for a consistent teaching logic. Full skill-by-skill drill tables are a separate training-content deliverable; this section records the adopted teaching principles.

**Tier 1 (ages 6–9, FUNdamentals) — teaching method:**
- One movement cue at a time, delivered with a short child-friendly phrase (e.g. "land like a ninja") rather than technical explanation.
- Stations and small-sided games instead of queues, to maximize repetitions per child.
- Progression by control and quality, not by increasing height/distance/speed — e.g. jumping progresses through better landing control before greater distance.
- Games-based delivery (tag, relays, obstacle courses, target games) rather than repetitive technical drilling.
- Observation checklists are explicitly for **deciding what to teach next, not for labeling a child "talented" or "untalented"** — reinforcing the no-premature-selection principle.
- Core skills covered: running, jumping/landing, throwing, catching, balance/stopping — common foundations across all four launch sports.

**Tier 2 (ages 9–12, Learn-to-Train) — teaching method:**
- Consistent cross-sport progression logic: simple → coordinated → moving → variable/opposed → modified game/event application.
- **Athletics:** run/jump/throw taught through World Athletics' Kids' Athletics game-based format (e.g. relays, target throws, jump-and-stick), not adult event specialization.
- **Badminton:** BWF-style foundation — movement/footwork before racket skill, hand-fed contact before rallying, modified court/net/shuttle before full-court play.
- **Boxing — strictly non-contact/controlled at this age.** Stance, guard, footwork, and basic straight punches on pads only, under close supervision. **Hard requirement, not a guideline:** no hard sparring, no deliberate head contact, no weight-cutting or dehydration practices, no normalizing aggression as identity — consistent with the safeguarding-first principle above.
- **Swimming:** Swim England-style progression — water confidence and safety first, then floating, push-and-glide, kicking, basic arm action, breathing, whole-stroke coordination. Should be delivered as a dedicated aquatic session with qualified supervision, not folded into a generic dry-land multisport circuit.

### Target Launch Geography — decided (30 Aug 2026)

**Primary focus: Tier-2 / Tier-2.5 cities**, not Tier-1. Rationale: Tier-1 metros (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Pune) already have multiple competing hybrid-academy players (Game Theory, Machaxi, Sportit, Peak Athlete) targeting the same affluent parent base; Tier-2/2.5 is both less contested and closer to Rit's original grassroots-access mission. Tier-1 remains a secondary/parallel target, not excluded.

**Candidate: the Haryana belt (Bhiwani, Sonepat, Panipat, Karnal, Hisar).** Chosen over Patiala (Punjab, strong NSNIS coach-education ecosystem) and Nagpur/Nashik (Maharashtra, strongest KIYG-performing state) because it directly covers three of Rit's four launch sports with existing institutional roots — Bhiwani is a nationally known boxing hub ("Mini Cuba," produced Vijender Singh), Sonepat hosts the SAI Northern Regional Centre, and the belt has strong existing athletics/wrestling tradition — and sits inside the state whose nursery + scholarship + residential-academy model was already identified as the strongest real-world "continuity" case study (Problem Statement 5). Swimming is the weaker link in this geography, which is mitigated by the pond/lake SOP approach already defined above.

**Known trade-off:** Tier-2/2.5 will likely have a thinner existing pool of already-credentialed coaches to onboard directly, compared to a metro — meaning coach acquisition and the coach-training module carry more early weight here than they would in a Tier-1 launch, since Rit may need to help create qualified coaches, not just onboard existing ones.

### Business Model — pivoted to B2B (30 Aug 2026)

**This supersedes the original parent-paid Free/Basic/Intermediate/Advance subscription model in Section 1.** As the product direction shifted from a training-content subscription to a coach + institution-first coaching-quality/talent-ID layer, the business model has correspondingly shifted from **B2C (parents pay)** to **B2B (coaches/schools/academies pay)**. Parents and athletes remain users of the platform (via the Athlete and Parent/Administrator access tiers already defined), but are not necessarily the primary payer.

**Not yet decided — deliberately deferred (30 Aug 2026):** the specific B2B pricing structure. Team Rit's explicit priority is building the best product first; monetization structure will be revisited once product-market fit is clearer, rather than being locked in prematurely. Relevant competitor benchmarks remain on record for when this discussion resumes: Sportit prices school-level SaaS at ~₹1/child/day (~₹365/child/year), with a 500-child minimum cohort; Peak Athlete prices at ₹10/student/month.

# 34. Core Principles

Rit should follow these principles throughout development.

### 1. Problem before technology

Do not select a problem because it gives us an opportunity to use AI.

### 2. Evidence before assumptions

Important decisions should be supported by research or user evidence.

### 3. Users before features

Features exist to solve user problems.

### 4. MVP before scale

First prove the core value.

### 5. AI where useful

Use AI only when it produces meaningful additional value.

### 6. Simplicity before complexity

Do not introduce unnecessary microservices, AI models, databases or infrastructure.

### 7. Measure before expanding

User behavior should determine the roadmap.

### 8. Build for India first

Understand Indian constraints rather than simply copying Western fitness products.

### 9. Design for impact

The ultimate goal is healthier and more active youth, not increased app usage.

### 10. Keep the business model flexible

Do not prematurely decide whether Rit will be B2C, B2B, B2G or open-source/public infrastructure.
