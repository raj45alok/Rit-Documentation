# 11 — Government Partnership & Procurement Landscape (India)

> **Purpose:** Understand how private sports-tech/ed-tech companies actually engage with Indian government sports institutions — to avoid incorrect assumptions, not to pursue government partnership as a current strategy. Directly validates an existing product guardrail.

---

## 1. Headline Finding

**No specific case study of a private sports-tech company partnering with Indian government sports bodies on a technology basis was identified.** Government partnerships found in this research were overwhelmingly **infrastructure or accreditation-focused** (academy accreditation, grant-funded facility construction), not technology/software partnerships. This is a genuine market gap — but also means there's no proven playbook to follow.

---

## 2. What Partnership Mechanisms Exist

| Mechanism | How it works |
|---|---|
| MoU with MYAS | Standard grantee agreement format for Khelo India-funded projects |
| Tenders (RFPs) | Via eProcure.gov.in — for program partners, service providers, event organizers, equipment supply |
| GeM (Government e-Marketplace) | Standard government procurement channel — not found in use for sports-tech specifically in this research |
| Accreditation | Academies can become "Khelo India accredited" — a recognition/funding relationship, not a technology partnership |
| CSR-funded deployment | Corporate CSR arms funding school/academy programs — an indirect path that doesn't require direct government procurement |

### Existing examples (infrastructure/training-content, not tech-platform)
- **Khelo India e-Pathshala** — collaboration with National Sports Federations for training *content*, not a third-party technology platform
- **SAI + CBSE/CISCE** — partnership to train PE teachers on the government's own Khelo India Mobile App (i.e., government building its own tool, not integrating a private one)

---

## 3. What This Means for Rit

### Realistic pathways (if pursued later)
1. Accreditation pathway — Rit-trained coaches/academies could pursue Khelo India accreditation independently, as a byproduct of quality, not as a Rit-government partnership per se
2. Training-content partnership — analogous to e-Pathshala's NSF collaboration model, if Rit's coach curriculum reaches sufficient credibility
3. Pilot programs — direct partnership with individual schools/academies (already the plan) rather than top-down government integration
4. Tender participation — only viable once relevant technology RFPs are actually issued, which none currently are for this specific space
5. CSR-funded deployment — a genuinely viable near-term path worth keeping in mind, distinct from formal government procurement

### What should NOT be assumed
- **Automatic government adoption** — no evidence this happens without a formal, often slow process
- **Fast procurement** — government procurement processes are documented as slow/bureaucratic generally
- **Data sharing** — no evidence government data (e.g., KIRTI/NSRS records) would be shared with a private platform without a formal agreement
- **Immediate profitability** — government contracts, where they exist, are not documented as an early revenue source

---

## 4. Direct Recommendation — Confirms an Existing Decision

**This research directly validates a guardrail already written into the Product Specification (Section 8, Guardrail #9): "Do not make government partnership a product dependency."**

Rit's MVP should proceed exactly as already planned — build and validate with real coaches/institutions first (the Haryana belt pilot), treat government partnership (KIRTI/NCAB integration) as a long-term aspiration contingent on Rit demonstrating a genuine track record, not a near-term requirement. See `RIT_PLAN.md`, "Relationship to KIRTI" section — the "long-term aspiration, not a near-term commitment" framing already anticipated exactly this finding.

### What evidence would eventually be needed before approaching government
Proven pilot track record and adoption metrics; demonstrated data security/privacy compliance; evidence of scalability; cost-effectiveness relative to government budget constraints.
