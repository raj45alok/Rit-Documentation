# 04 — Competitor Analysis: India's Child Sports-Tech Market

> **Purpose:** Market map of existing digital platforms in India's child sports/fitness space, their pricing, claims, and — most importantly — what independent evidence (if any) exists for each. This directly informs Rit's differentiation strategy.

---

## 1. The Core Finding

**No independently published controlled study was identified, across any platform below, showing improved activity levels, retention, skill development, or talent-prediction accuracy compared to standard PE or coaching.** Every "AI-powered," "scientific," or outcome claim reviewed is a company-reported marketing claim, not independent evidence. This is the single most important market finding: **credibility itself is unclaimed territory.**

## 3. Category Analysis

### Government assessment (Khelo India App)
The most important non-commercial player. Free, official, real fitness-assessment function (8 tests, starting age 5). **Caution:** the claim that fitness tests can identify "which sport a child can excel in" is a government program claim, not independently proven predictive accuracy. Rit's Tier 1/2 assessment should be understood as complementary to, not duplicating, this free layer.

### School SaaS (Sportit, Peak Athlete)
Address a real operational gap (Indian schools lack consistent PE lesson plans/assessment/data). Sportit is the more established; Peak Athlete's website shows reliability red flags (simultaneously displaying zero-value and positive-traction metrics) — a buyer-diligence lesson for how Rit should NOT present its own metrics.

### Direct-to-parent online (KidPRO, Striide, Chhote Champs)
Parent-paid, live/online delivery. Striide is notable as the only platform with a **girls-specific** design — internationally sourced, cricket-only, no independent India evidence yet.

### Hybrid academy/membership (Game Theory, Machaxi, Durosport, Sarai Sports)
Offline coaching with digital booking/tracking layers. These occupy **the same price bracket Rit's original B2C tiers were designed for** (₹500–20,000/year) — a key reason the plan pivoted toward B2B/Tier-2 positioning to avoid direct Tier-1 competition.

### Discovery/booking (Play A Sport, Playo)
Logistics tools, not development platforms — lowest relevance to Rit's actual differentiation.

## 4. Due-Diligence Framework (for evaluating any platform, including Rit's own)

Before trusting any child-sports platform, verify:
1. **Child safety** — coach identity/credentials, safeguarding policy, supervision ratios, reporting channels
2. **Coach quality** — actual qualification (B.P.Ed./NSNIS/federation), first-aid competence, age-band experience
3. **Age appropriateness** — separate plans for 6–8, 9–11, 12–16, not one generic program
4. **Assessment validity** — exact tests used, normative data source, who reviews flagged results
5. **Data governance** — what's collected (video, health data, location), storage, deletion/consent process
6. **Commercial clarity** — full costs including GST, minimum commitment, refund rules

---

## 5. What This Means for Rit's Differentiation

- No competitor has independently verified methodology — Rit's use of **published, validated batteries** (PERF-FIT, KTK3+, OSF) with **versioned, auditable results** is a genuine, checkable differentiator, not a marketing claim.
- No competitor is positioned as **complementary to government infrastructure** (NCAB/KIRTI) — every one operates as if government systems don't exist.
- Swimming is under-served across the competitor set generally, reinforcing it as relatively open ground.
- Girls-specific design (Striide) is real whitespace that Rit has deliberately chosen not to pursue as the core wedge (see `RIT_PLAN.md` Strategic Direction) — worth revisiting if evidence from the pilot suggests otherwise.
