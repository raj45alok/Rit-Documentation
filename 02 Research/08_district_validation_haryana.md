# 08 — District-Level Validation: Haryana-Specific Evidence

> **Purpose:** The closest available proxy to primary research for Rit's target geography. No study directly interviews Haryana parents/coaches about coaching quality specifically in athletics/badminton/boxing/swimming — this document presents the nearest existing evidence and the decision to proceed without commissioning fresh primary research at this stage.

---

## 1. Confirmed Evidence Gap

**No nationally representative, peer-reviewed qualitative study directly examines parents, coaches, or PE teachers in Haryana or nearby Tier-2 cities about youth-sports coaching quality, talent identification, or participation specifically in athletics, badminton, boxing, or swimming.** Available evidence consists of small-scale survey studies, government program descriptions, and evaluation reports — useful for study design and directional validation, not definitive state-level findings.

---

## 2. The Single Most Important Finding: Coaching-Service Perception Study

**Bhiwani / Hisar / Jind, 2015 (n=180, 60 trainees per district)**

| District | "Coach is skilled" | "Coach tracks training schedule" | "Coach gives technique feedback" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bhiwani** | 93.33% agree | 41.67% agree | 46.67% agree |
| **Hisar** | 90% agree | 43.33% agree | 33.33% agree |
| **Jind** | 91.67% agree | 46.67% agree (goals/plans: 58.33%) | 46.67% agree |

**Interpretation — this is the sharpest, most directly useful finding in the entire research base:**

Trainees overwhelmingly perceive their coaches as *skilled* (90%+ across all three districts). But systematic tracking and structured feedback is weak everywhere — **including in Bhiwani**, Rit's chosen boxing hub and the best-perceived of the three districts.

**This directly validates Rit's chosen product wedge:** the gap is not "coaches need to be more skilled." The gap is that already-skilled coaches lack tracking/feedback infrastructure. This is a sharper, more defensible, and more specific thesis than a generic "coaching quality" framing.

*Limitations: convenience sampling, self-made questionnaire, no independent quality audit, no sport-specific breakdown across Rit's four target sports.*

## 3. Parent Attitude Studies (Haryana-Specific)

### SPAT (Sports and Physical Aptitude Test) Parents — Rohtak, 2013 (n=50)
| Finding | % |
|---|---|
| Considered sport a better career for their child | 68% |
| Believed trainers/coaches had full potential to train | 80% |
| Felt long training sessions hurt studies | 56% |
| Satisfied with scholarship amount | 44% |
| **Dissatisfied with SPAT selection procedure** | **60%** |
| **Believed training centres lacked latest facilities** | **82%** |
| Wanted special school-based training before SPAT | 96% |
| Wanted gender-matched coaches | 78% |
| Wanted government accommodation for trainees | 92% |
| Thought rural people weren't fully aware of SPAT | 80% |

**Relevance to Rit:** the 60% dissatisfaction with selection-procedure fairness and 82% facility-quality concern directly support Problem Statement 7 (no credible way to verify quality/fairness before committing). The 78% demand for gender-matched coaches is a real signal worth keeping as a coach-registry *feature option* (letting parents filter/request), even though Rit has deliberately chosen not to make gender-specific design the core wedge.

*Limitations: small (n=50), non-representative, single district, self-designed questionnaire.*

### Female Sports Participation — Rohtak, 2017 (n=200)
Higher-socioeconomic-status parents more likely to encourage daughters' sports participation. Parents generally encourage girls' participation for health/lifestyle reasons, but ethnicity, culture, and physical constraints remain barriers. *Does not address coaching quality or talent-ID.*

### Female Athletes — Sirsa, 2024 (n=104)
- 81.7% strongly agreed PE/sports are important for girls
- 66.3% strongly agreed they encourage daughters' participation
- 59.6% strongly agreed they would support a professional sports career for their daughter
- Only **42.3%** agreed there are enough sports opportunities for girls in their community (25% disagreed, 13.5% strongly disagreed)

*Does not evaluate coaching quality or talent-ID practices.*

## 4. Talent-Scheme Evaluation

### Wrestling Talent Search Scheme — Haryana, 2019
Interviews with 50 PE teachers, wrestling coaches, wrestlers, and school/college principals. Concluded the scheme helped identify and nurture wrestling talent — but did not provide detailed quantitative evaluation of coaching quality or cross-sport talent-ID criteria. *Wrestling-specific; not directly transferable to Rit's four sports.*

---

## 5. Scale Context — Government Training Already Delivered

**82,000+ PE teachers** have already been trained nationally via Khelo India specifically to conduct basic fitness assessment via the mobile app. This is an important refinement to the Coach Credentialing plan (`02_coach_credentialing.md`): Rit should not assume it is training coaches from zero — many already hold baseline assessment training. Rit's value-add is the standardized orientation battery, tracking/feedback layer, safeguarding, and LTAD-informed pedagogy **layered on top of** this existing baseline.

---

## 6. Decision: Proceed Without Commissioning Fresh Primary Research

**Rationale:** the district-level evidence above, combined with the extensive secondary research in documents 01–07, is treated as sufficient directional validation for MVP-stage product decisions. This is a **deliberate, acknowledged acceptance of residual validation risk** — not an oversight.

**Revisit this decision if:** early pilot feedback (once Rit is actually operating in the Haryana belt) contradicts the district-level findings above — particularly the core "skilled but no tracking/feedback" thesis.

---

## 7. Implications for a Future Primary-Research Study (if commissioned later)

If Team Rit later decides a dedicated study is warranted:
- Use semi-structured interviews/focus groups with parents, grassroots coaches, and PE teachers in Rohtak, Hisar, Panipat, Sonepat, or Karnal specifically
- Ask specifically about athletics, badminton, boxing, and swimming — not generic "sports"
- Triangulate with facility audits, coach-qualification checks, and program data (KIC/HSA nursery records)
- Report limitations clearly (sample size, selection bias, single-state context)
