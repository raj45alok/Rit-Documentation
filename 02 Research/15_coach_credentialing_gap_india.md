# 15 — Coach Credentialing Gap (India): Resolved Findings

> **Purpose:** This document resolves the open research question flagged in `02_coach_credentialing.md` ("How are grassroots youth coaches in India currently trained, assessed, certified, verified, and monitored, and where are the specific gaps?"). It also resolves the Direction A vs. B fork with an evidence-based answer rather than a risk-preference judgment.

---

## 1. The Fragmented-But-Real Landscape (Confirmed)

Multiple parallel qualification pathways coexist with **no single mandatory national licence**:

| Qualification | Provider | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Diploma/Certificate in Sports Coaching | NSNIS/SAI | Current, verified |
| M.Sc. in Sports Coaching | NSNIS Patiala | Current, verified |
| Khelo India E-Khel Pathshala | MYAS/Khelo India | Current, verified |
| NSF sport-specific courses | Boxing/Badminton/Athletics/Swimming Federations | Current, verified (content varies) |
| State department courses | State sports departments | Current, verified |
| University PE/sports science degrees | LNIPE, sports universities | Current, verified |
| NSDC/NSQF-linked qualifications | Sector skill councils | Current, verified in principle |
| Private academy certificates | Commercial providers | Current, verified as existing; **quality/recognition varies widely** |

## 3. Major New Finding: The Task Force's Actual Proposed Scale

**The December 2025 task force proposes 1,400 hours of practical training + 400 hours of theory** — a "Practice–Theory–Practice" model. This is **dramatically larger** than the 20-40 hour international-framework hypothesis previously logged in `02_coach_credentialing.md`. This is a significant correction: if NCAB's actual proposed standard is ~1,800 total hours, that reinforces — strongly — that Rit should not attempt to build or deliver training at this scale itself.

### Full NCAB proposal (as currently defined)
- National Coach Accreditation Board (NCAB)
- Three-tier levels: Grassroots, Intermediate, Elite, plus Community Coaches and PE Teachers
- Unified national curriculum with sport-specific adaptations
- 1,400 practical hours + 400 theory hours
- National coaches registry with 3-year licenses
- CPD requirements for renewal
- Independent Safe Sport oversight linked to licensing
- **Digital coach passport** tracking qualifications, CPD, and background checks
- Regional Centres of Coaching Excellence
- Coach support and grievance cell

**Status: 100% of the above remains PROPOSED, not implemented.** Existing NSNIS/SAI courses and Khelo India E-Khel Pathshala are the only currently operational (verified) components.

## 4. Direction A vs. B — Now Resolved by Evidence

The research gives a direct, unambiguous recommendation:

> **"Rit should verify existing credentials and provide coaching-quality infrastructure, rather than duplicating training."**

This resolves the fork logged in `02_coach_credentialing.md` and the Product Specification. Combined with the 1,400+400 hour finding above, attempting Direction A (Rit teaching/certifying coaches from scratch) is now clearly the wrong call — not just higher-risk, but actually redundant with (and far smaller in scale than) what NCAB is already proposing to build.

**Rit's coach module should be built as:**
1. **Credential verification/registry (Direction B)** — display a coach's existing qualifications, verification status, and safeguarding compliance
2. **Coaching-quality infrastructure** — the assessment/tracking/feedback tooling already core to the plan (this is not "training," it's operational tooling layered on top of whatever qualification a coach already holds)
3. **A lightweight complementary learning layer** (already scoped correctly in Product Spec 4.6: "should complement, not replace, formal government/federation qualifications") — this remains appropriate precisely *because* it doesn't attempt to replace the 1,800-hour NCAB proposal; it fills specific gaps (session planning, feedback methodology, safeguarding awareness) rather than functioning as an alternative certification pathway

### What NOT to build (explicit finding)
- Duplicate entry-level coaching courses
- Duplicate national licensing (until/unless NCAB actually becomes operational and a formal integration makes sense)

---

## 5. Verification Reality Today

**No unified public portal exists where a parent/school can check a coach's credentials, CPD status, and disciplinary history across all sports and providers.** Verification today is fragmented: checking certificates directly, checking NSNIS alumni status where applicable, checking federation registries where they exist. **This is the actual, confirmed market gap Direction B fills** — not a hypothetical one.

---

## 6. What India Already Does Well (Do Not Duplicate)

- NSNIS provides structured diploma/certificate/M.Sc. pathways
- Khelo India E-Khel Pathshala offers scalable, standardized online+physical training
- NSFs and state departments run sport-specific courses
- SAI employs qualified coaches at its own centres

---

## 7. Updated Recommendation for Rit's MVP

1. Focus on coach **verification**, not training delivery
2. Build the coaching-quality/tracking infrastructure (already core to Phase 1 per the Product Specification)
3. Prioritize safeguarding and parent-consent flows as load-bearing features, not secondary ones
4. Ensure DPDP Act compliance with verifiable parental consent (see `14_child_data_privacy_safeguarding.md`)
5. Do not attempt to replicate or compete with NCAB's proposed 1,800-hour training model
6. Revisit formal NCAB integration only once NCAB is actually operational — continue treating it as a long-term aspiration, not a Phase 1 dependency (consistent with the existing "Relationship to KIRTI" positioning in `RIT_PLAN.md`)
