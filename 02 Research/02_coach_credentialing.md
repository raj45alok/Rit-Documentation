# 02 — Coach Credentialing: Current State (India) & International Frameworks

> **Purpose:** Evidence base for Rit's coach-credentialing module design. Covers what India's coaching workforce currently looks like, what the government's own reform proposal (NCAB) contains, and what international frameworks offer as design patterns.
>
> **Important caveat:** International framework parameters (hours, session counts, renewal periods) are **design guidance from other countries, not validated evidence of what India specifically needs.** Treat as working hypotheses pending dedicated India-specific research (see Open Research Question at the end of this document).

---

## 1. Current State — India

### PE Teacher qualification routes (NCTE-regulated)
| Qualification | Level | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| C.P.Ed. (Certificate) | Elementary PE | Requirements vary by state; certificate holders may lack depth for secondary-level roles |
| D.P.Ed. (Diploma) | Elementary PE | Availability and employer acceptance uneven |
| B.P.Ed. (Bachelor's) | Classes VI–X primary; XI–XII activity roles | A teaching qualification, not automatically a talent-ID or sport-specific coaching credential |
| M.P.Ed. (Master's) | Advanced/supervisory | Does not replace sport-specific coaching education |

### Grassroots coach routes
- **NSNIS (Netaji Subhas National Institute of Sports)**, under SAI: Diploma in Sports Coaching + 6-week certificate courses
- Federation-run and private/international certifications — **the Dec 2025 task force explicitly flags this proliferation as a source of confusion, not clarity**
- Result: a child may encounter a B.P.Ed. teacher, an NSNIS-trained coach, a former athlete with no formal qualification, a federation-certified coach, or an unverified volunteer — with no way to distinguish these from outside.

### Scale of existing training (important context)
- **82,000+ PE teachers** already trained nationally via Khelo India specifically to conduct **fitness assessment** (not talent-ID, not sport-specific coaching)
- This means: Rit's value-add should be understood as the standardized orientation battery + tracking/feedback layer + safeguarding + LTAD pedagogy **layered on top of** existing baseline assessment training — not "coach training from zero."

### The government's own diagnosis (Dec 2025 Coaching Ecosystem Task Force)
Direct findings, not inferred:
- India lacks sufficient quality coaches at **grassroots, intermediate, and elite levels**
- Existing development is **"fragmented, inconsistent, and overly dependent on individual effort rather than durable institutions"**
- The proliferation of courses/programs/certifications has **"caused confusion rather than a clear national quality standard"**

## 2. National Coach Accreditation Board (NCAB) — Proposed Reform

**Status as of mid-2026: actively being built, not yet nationally live.**

- Led by a task force under Pullela Gopichand
- Includes: National Coach Registry, dedicated Accreditation Portal, standards explicitly described as **LTAD-based**
- Currently in **pilot phase with 3–4 National Sports Federations**
- Includes a "TOPS for Coaches" support scheme

### Proposed tier structure
| Tier | Function |
|---|---|
| Community coach / PE-teacher feeder | Broad participation, physical literacy |
| Grassroots coach | Early talent identification, age-appropriate development |
| Intermediate coach | State/national competition preparation |
| Elite coach | International/professional performance support |
| National-team coach | India-level international preparation |

### Strategic implication for Rit
**Do not build Rit as a competing accreditation authority.** NCAB is explicitly designed to be the official national one and is philosophically LTAD-aligned with Rit's own framework. Rit's coach module should be **NCAB-compatible infrastructure** — training/tracking content mapped onto NCAB's tiers, ready to plug in once NCAB is nationally live — not a rival system. See `RIT_PLAN.md`, "Relationship to KIRTI" and "Coach Credential Model" sections for the full positioning decision.

## 3. International Frameworks (Design Guidance — Not Yet India-Validated)

Cross-checked: ISCF/ICCE-ASOIF, UK Coaching, England Athletics, England Squash, World Rugby, BWF.

### Common architecture across mature systems
```
Orientation/Assistant level → Entry-level coach (Level 1) → Development coach (Level 2) →
Performance coach → High-performance/master coach
```

### Consistent design features
- **Safeguarding is a mandatory prerequisite** in every system reviewed — not optional add-on content
- **Blended format:** online modules + tutor-led workshop + supervised field practice + portfolio
- **Competency-based assessment** (competent / not-yet-competent), not attendance-based certification
- **Renewable license** (2–3 years typically), tied to safeguarding recertification and CPD
- **Public/verifiable coach registry** — ID, qualification level, sport, expiry, safeguarding status

### ISCF's three knowledge domains
| Domain | Content |
|---|---|
| Professional knowledge | Sport foundations, child development, session planning, safety, ethics |
| Interpersonal knowledge | Communication, relationship-building, inclusive group management |
| Intrapersonal knowledge | Reflection, self-awareness, knowing when to refer/seek help |

### Safeguarding curriculum content (consistent across systems)
Recognizing harm indicators, professional boundaries, responding to disclosure, reporting/escalation pathways, safe operations (ratios, sign-in/out, transport, photography/consent), inclusion and dignity, parent communication protocols, record-keeping.

## 4. India-Specific Additions (Beyond Generic Imported Frameworks)

These do not appear in standard international curricula and are proposed as India-specific requirements:
- Heat/humidity/air-pollution/monsoon risk management
- Low-equipment and small-space session design
- Gender-sensitive coaching and girls' safe participation
- Child-safety and digital-communication (WhatsApp-era) boundaries
- Inclusion across disability, language, caste/community, income, rural/urban access
- Parent communication around academic-pressure trade-offs and realistic sport-career expectations
- Prevention of early specialization, overtraining, unsafe weight-control/supplement practices
- Referral procedures for suspected injury, anaemia/fatigue, undernutrition, mental-health concerns
- **Explicit, trained separation between fitness screening and talent selection** — with safeguards against premature exclusion

## 5. Direction A vs. Direction B — Two Different Potential Products

An internal review flagged that "Coach Module" actually conflates two distinct products:

| | Direction A: Certification/Training | Direction B: Verification/Trust Infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Rit teaches, assesses, and credentials coaches | Rit aggregates/displays a coach's *existing* qualifications, safeguarding status, experience |
| Risk profile | Higher — Rit owns curriculum design and pedagogical liability | Lower — lighter-weight registry/display layer |
| Answers | "How do I become a better coach?" | "Can I trust this coach with my child?" (parent/school question) |

**Decision: pursue both, sequenced/parallel — not either/or.** Direction B is faster and lower-risk. But the strongest local evidence (see `08_district_validation_haryana.md`) shows coaches are already perceived as skilled — the actual gap is tracking/feedback, which is closer to a Direction-A-adjacent capability ("give already-skilled coaches a tool," not "teach from zero"). Both matter; neither replaces the other.

## 6. Open Research Question (Priority — Not Yet Answered)

**"How are grassroots youth coaches in India currently trained, assessed, certified, verified, and monitored, and where are the specific gaps?"**

Break into:
- Existing certification pathways (state-by-state)
- Actual curriculum content (not just course titles)
- Practical assessment methods in current use
- Safeguarding requirements as currently enforced
- Background verification practices
- First-aid requirements
- CPD/renewal enforcement
- Coach registry existence (state vs. national)
- Disciplinary mechanisms
- Sport-specific requirement differences
- What parents/schools can actually verify **today**, in practice

This should be run as a dedicated Deep Research pass before finalizing exact curriculum parameters (hours, session counts, renewal periods) in the product build.
